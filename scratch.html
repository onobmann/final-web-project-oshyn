Why I Run

Running started as a way to clear my head between study sessions. What began as reluctant 20-minute jogs evolved into something I genuinely crave—early mornings before campus wakes up, the smell of rain on asphalt, the particular silence of mile four when everything stops hurting and your mind finally goes quiet.

Running teaches you the same lessons over and over: that discomfort is temporary, that progress is non-linear, and that showing up on the hard days matters more than anything you do on the easy ones. Those lessons live in everything else I do.

8-Week Base Training Plan

Whether you're training for your first 5K or building your aerobic base for a marathon, consistency beats intensity every time. Here's the structure I follow—and the gear that makes each phase possible.

  1. Easy Running (Weeks 1–2) Run at a conversational pace — you should be able to hold a full sentence. This builds your aerobic engine without burning you out. Aim for 3–4 runs per week.
    • Cushioned daily trainer (Brooks Ghost, ASICS Gel-Nimbus)
    • Moisture-wicking running socks
    • GPS watch or running app (Garmin, Strava)
  2. Tempo Runs (Weeks 3–4) Run at "comfortably hard" — you can speak a few words but not full sentences. These runs build your lactate threshold and teach your body to sustain faster paces.
    • Racing flat or tempo shoe (Nike Pegasus, Saucony Kinvara)
    • Heart rate monitor or chest strap
    • Foam roller for recovery
  3. Long Runs (Weeks 5–6) The foundation of any distance training program. Run your longest run of the week at an easy, relaxed pace. Add 10% distance week-over-week at most.
    • Hydration vest or handheld water bottle
    • Energy gels or chews (every 45–60 min past 60 min total)
    • Anti-chafe balm (Body Glide)
    • Sun protection: hat, SPF 50 sunscreen
  4. Speed Work (Weeks 7–8) Track intervals, hill repeats, and strides build raw speed and neuromuscular efficiency. These are intense—keep them to one session per week maximum.
    • Lightweight racing flat or spike (for track)
    • Track or measured route
    • Interval timer app (WorkOutDoors, Intervals Pro)
  5. Recovery Week After every 3 hard weeks, take a recovery week: cut volume by 30–40%. Sleep, stretch, and let your body absorb the adaptations from the previous weeks.
    • Yoga mat and stretching routine
    • Ice bath or contrast shower
    • Sleep tracker (Oura Ring, Whoop)

Things I've Learned About Running

80/20 Rule

Elite runners spend ~80% of their training at low intensity and only 20% at high intensity. Most beginners invert this — and burn out.

Cadence Matters

The optimal stride rate is around 170–180 steps per minute regardless of pace. Higher cadence = less impact force = fewer injuries.

The Wall Is Real

At mile 18–20 in a marathon, glycogen stores deplete. Your body literally starts burning fat as fuel — which is much slower. Train your gut before race day.

Shoes Don't Prevent Injuries

Research shows that comfort — not cushioning type — is the strongest predictor of injury prevention. Wear what feels right.

Sleep Is Training

Growth hormone (used for muscle repair) is released almost entirely during deep sleep. Skipping sleep skips recovery. 8+ hours is non-negotiable for runners.

Mental > Physical

Studies show that when you feel like you're at 100% effort, you're actually at about 60% of your physiological maximum. Your brain quits before your body does.
